# v1.6 Mission Review — Synthesis

Reviewer: orchestrator-level synthesis (single pass against repo HEAD `e29c94d`).

Source mission: `docs/plans/v1.6-mission.md`.

---

## Executive verdict

**Ship with material edits.**

The mission's headline call — move the sim into a dedicated Web Worker, leave the main thread for render — is the right direction at the right time. v1.5's worker-stats panel and the dynamic-chunk NN pass already prove the parallel-NN substrate works; decoupling render is the next compounding win. The mission's three-stage path (postMessage → SAB → polish) is also the right shape.

But as written it has the same disease the v1.5 mission had: **load-bearing design choices are present in spirit but not in writing**, and **magic numbers don't fully survive arithmetic**. The implementer will hit five things on contact that the mission either glosses or contradicts:

1. **Where wasm lives, and how SAB is shared with it, is unspecified.** Mission says "Wasm crate code stays as-is. `WorldHandle` keeps its current API." It also says zero-copy SAB snapshot reads on main. Both cannot be true. Either (a) the worker holds wasm and the SAB is an *external* `SharedArrayBuffer` that wasm must learn to write into (API change), or (b) the SAB *is* the wasm linear memory and main constructs views over `world.memory.buffer` (no API change but a shared-memory wrinkle). The mission needs to pick.
2. **`pack_render_buffer` does frustum culling against `camera` Rust-side.** Today the renderer hands `cam_cx/cy/zoom/viewport` to wasm; wasm returns a GPU-ready instance buffer. Post-decoupling, the worker has no camera. The mission's SAB layout (`x, y, r, g, b, body_radius` per creature) is *unfrustumed*. The mission has silently moved the cull (and the GPU-instance-pack) to either JS or to per-frame `postMessage(camera)`. Both have non-trivial cost; neither is in the budget.
3. **Inspector "fast path id from snapshot" assumes the snapshot contains creature IDs. It doesn't.** Risk-table mitigation says click maps to nearest id locally — but the SAB layout in §"What lives in the SAB" omits the id buffer. Inconsistency between the architecture proposal and the risk-table workaround.
4. **`step_n(1)` per loop iteration burns wasm-bindgen call overhead at the top of the TPS dropdown.** `tpsOptions = [10, 30, 60, 180, 500, 1000]`. At 1000 TPS the loop overhead is ~5–50 µs × 1000 calls/sec = 5–50 ms/sec — the very cost main.ts amortizes today by calling `step_n(ticksThisFrame)`. Mission's tick loop must accumulate a batch.
5. **`setTimeout(0)` sleep does not pace at 500–1000 TPS.** The mission writes `sleep(max(0, budget - elapsed))` without naming the mechanism. The natural choice — `setTimeout(0)` — has a 4 ms minimum in Chrome and 4–10 ms after nested timer throttling. At 1000 TPS the budget is 1 ms. The pacing primitive must be `Atomics.wait` on a futex word (workers can; main can't). Mission silent.

**Two pleasant simplifications** the mission could lean into harder:

- The renderer is *already* DPR-capped, frustum-culled, R8-textured, and instanced. The "main thread shrinks to render loop + UI plumbing" claim is honest — and the actual code at `web/src/main.ts:199-260` is already small enough that the cut is shallower than the prose suggests. Mission undersells this.
- The v1.5 `nn_worker_stats_json` and `profile_report_json` poll cadences are 750 ms and ~1 Hz respectively. They are *not* per-frame and do not need SAB. A simple `postMessage` request/response works at this rate.

### Bottom line

- **Three-stage sequencing**: ship as designed.
- **SAB layout for grass + creature state**: ship — spec ID column and add a `current_idx` flip word.
- **Render snapshot bandwidth budget**: re-do the arithmetic with the wasm→SAB write cost included.
- **wasm-bindgen-rayon in a worker**: low-risk, but the mission's "Stage 1 verifies day one" needs a documented fallback that is *not* silent (the v1.5 single-threaded footgun cost the user a full debugging session — see `e29c94d`).
- **Pacing mechanism**: switch to `Atomics.wait` on a futex; document it.
- **Inspector / slider / restart message protocols**: spec each one; the mission says "Inspector becomes async" but doesn't name the message shapes, the staleness budget, or the rendezvous on restart.
- **Frustum culling + render-pack ownership**: pick one location and own the implications.

**Realistic time budget: 2.5–3 working days**, not 1–2. Stage 1 alone is a full day once Vite worker bundling, wasm-init-in-worker, message protocol, restart flow, slider routing, and inspector round-trip are all wired. Stage 2 is a full day if the SAB write path needs Rust changes (it does). Stage 3 is the only half-day stage. The mission's "If something hits an unforeseen rayon-in-worker bug, add a day" should be the *default* allowance, not the contingency.

## Strengths (where the mission is right)

- **The decoupling motivation is sharp.** "Sim TPS becomes independent of render FPS" is exactly the user-visible win, and the v1.5 instrumentation now actually lets us measure it. The "why now" timing is correct: the perf substrate is ready, the bottleneck is real, and SAB plumbing already works.
- **Three-stage sequencing is right.** postMessage payloads first to prove out the message protocol and restart flow without committing to a SAB layout; SAB second once the contract is stable; UX polish last. This is the same pattern v1.5 used (S3 EMA pipe before render swap; S5a topology before S5b inputs) and it worked.
- **Identifying the rayon-in-worker question as risk #1.** This is the highest-ROI uncertainty in the plan and the mission flags it correctly. The "fallback = single-threaded sim worker is still a strict win" framing is the right answer.
- **Restart-as-worker-respawn is honest.** Terminating the worker and spawning a fresh one with a new SAB is simpler than trying to reset a long-lived worker. The "~100 ms blip" is acceptable.
- **Render snapshot double-buffer with atomic flip is the correct concurrency primitive.** Locks would deadlock against the render-loop's RAF cadence; the atomic-store-on-flip-index keeps both sides lock-free.
- **Non-goals are well-drawn.** GPU grass, save/load, multi-world, inspector streaming — all correctly out of scope. The mission does not pretend this PR is bigger than it is.
- **Acceptance criterion #3 (the v1.5 footgun warning).** Explicitly demanding that the sim worker log `[sim] rayon workers: N` at startup — same as main does today — directly addresses the v1.5 silent-single-thread incident. Right reflex.

## Critical issues (must address before implementation)

### C1. The wasm-instance / SAB-ownership question is unresolved

**Source:** Mission §"What does NOT change" claims `WorldHandle` API is unchanged; §"What lives in the SAB" + Stage 2 imply zero-copy SAB reads on main. These are mutually exclusive.

Two coherent paths exist; the mission must pick one:

**Path A — separate SAB, single wasm instance in the worker.**
- Main holds no wasm; just SAB-backed `Float32Array`/`Uint8Array` views over the snapshot.
- Worker's wasm copies (or atomic-stores) snapshot data into the external SAB at end of each tick.
- Implication: `WorldHandle` API changes. `creatures_buffer()` and `grass_buffer_u8()` cannot return their current `Float32Array::view(&self.creature_buf)` because the buffer they need to write into lives in a SAB outside wasm linear memory. The Rust side needs a new `write_snapshot_to(&mut [u8])` API or equivalent — wasm-bindgen passes a `js_sys::Uint8Array` constructed over the SAB, the Rust side does `dst.copy_from(&src)`.
- Cost: extra Rust→JS copy on every tick. At pop=2000 it's 280 KB × 60 TPS = 16.8 MB/s. The v1.5 R8 grass path budgeted 14 MB/s for *one* buffer alone (`DECISIONS.md:426`). Plausible but not "below noise floor."

**Path B — shared wasm linear memory, two wasm-bindgen instances.**
- Worker constructs a `WebAssembly.Memory({shared: true, ...})` and instantiates wasm against it. Main instantiates the *same* wasm module against the *same* memory.
- Snapshot lives in wasm linear memory; main constructs `Float32Array(world.memory.buffer, ptr, len)` views directly. No copies.
- Implication: no `WorldHandle` API change in spirit, but the boot sequence has two wasm-bindgen contexts holding handles to the same memory, and you have to pass the memory + shared module across the worker boundary at startup. Doable; non-trivial; `wasm-bindgen-rayon`'s `workerHelpers` already does exactly this for rayon workers, so the precedent is in-repo.
- Cost: GC / lifetime semantics get hairier (main's `Float32Array` view points into worker-owned memory; if the worker terminates and respawns on restart, those views become detached). Restart flow needs a careful sequence.

The mission's prose floats between these. **Pick Path A**: it's strictly simpler, requires no main-thread wasm, and the 16.8 MB/s write cost is fine. State this explicitly in §"High-level architecture" and re-budget Stage 2's "replace postMessage with SAB" to include the Rust-side `write_snapshot_to(&Uint8Array)` API.

### C2. Frustum culling + `pack_render_buffer` has no spec'd home post-decoupling

**Source:** `src/wasm_api.rs:236-294` (`pack_render_buffer` takes camera, frustum-culls, returns GPU instance layout). Mission §"What lives in the SAB" enumerates `x, y, r, g, b, body_radius` — unfrustumed, not GPU layout.

Today the wasm side produces the exact `[cx, cy, outer_px, inner_px, r, g, b, a]` byte stream the GPU consumes, with the off-screen creatures already culled. Three viable post-decoupling architectures:

1. **Worker posts unfrustumed SoA; main culls + packs in JS.** Simplest, undoes the v1.5 perf wins. At pop=2000, the JS cull+pack loop was the cost we paid Rust to eliminate.
2. **Main `postMessage`s camera every frame; worker writes a culled, GPU-ready SAB.** Round-trip latency = 1 RAF frame; render-snapshot now reflects two-frame-old camera. Acceptable but visibly laggy at pan/zoom.
3. **Worker writes unfrustumed SoA into one SAB; main does cull-only in JS, GPU does the rest.** SoA is small enough at pop≤2000 that JS cull is cheap (it's just two compares per creature); GPU still gets a culled instance buffer because main fills the instance buffer scratch. Loses Rust's tight pack loop but keeps the render pipeline.

Pick (3): the per-frame Rust→SAB write becomes "raw SoA" and the renderer reclaims the cull. The cost is ~30 µs JS at pop=2000 (2000 × ~15 ns per cull check + a Float32Array.set into instanceScratch). The mission needs to spec which path and update the snapshot layout accordingly. The current "48 KB per pop=2000" math implies (3) — fine, but the GPU-pack cost should be acknowledged.

### C3. The snapshot's creature ID column is missing

**Source:** Mission §"What lives in the SAB" lists `Creature SoA (x, y, r, g, b, body_radius)`; §"Risks + mitigations" / Inspector latency says "the live snapshot already contains a per-creature `id` array; 'click' maps to nearest id locally."

These contradict. Click → id resolution is essential because the inspector tracks creatures by stable ID, not array index (post-`step()`, indices shift). The id is a `u64` (8 bytes); adding it to the snapshot grows the per-creature stride from 24 → 32 bytes. At pop=2000 the creature snapshot grows from 48 KB → 64 KB. Negligible cost; meaningful correctness gap.

Spec it explicitly: snapshot layout per creature = `[x: f32, y: f32, r: f32, g: f32, b: f32, body_radius: f32, id_lo: u32, id_hi: u32]`. The `id_hi` is always 0 in v1 (IDs stay <2^53; see `wasm_api.rs:586-592`), so the click-to-id reduction is `id_lo` + a JS-side `(hi * 2^32 + lo)`.

### C4. `step_n(1)` per loop iteration negates wasm-bindgen call amortization

**Source:** Mission §"High-level architecture" sim worker pseudocode: `world.step_n(1)`. Main.ts today: `world.step_n(ticksThisFrame)` (`web/src/main.ts:235`).

`step_n` exists precisely because per-call wasm-bindgen overhead (typed-array marshalling, `getrandom` thread-local lookup, profiler push/pop) is ~5–50 µs per call. Today at 60 TPS the main loop calls it ~60×/sec, batched. At 1000 TPS (top of the dropdown) the mission's `step_n(1)` would mean 1000 calls/sec = 5–50 ms/sec just on glue overhead, on top of actual sim cost.

Fix: accumulate tick budget *inside* the worker the same way main.ts does today (`web/src/main.ts:222-232`):

```
loop:
  budget += targetTPS * (now - lastTickTime) / 1000
  batch = min(floor(budget), MAX_BATCH)
  budget -= batch
  if batch > 0: world.step_n(batch); writeSnapshot()
  atomicWait(until next budget-tick OR slider-message)
```

The "write snapshot only after the batch" detail also matters — writing per-tick is unnecessary if the batch is N ticks and main only paints once per RAF anyway. Snapshot at end of batch is correct.

### C5. Pacing primitive is unspecified; `setTimeout(0)` will not pace at the top of the TPS dropdown

**Source:** Mission §"High-level architecture" `sleep(max(0, budget - elapsed))`. No mechanism named.

Chrome enforces a 4 ms minimum for `setTimeout(0)`. Nested timers throttle to 4–10 ms. At targetTPS=1000, the per-tick budget is 1 ms; `setTimeout` cannot hit this rate even with empty work. Targets ≥250 TPS likely degrade.

Workers can use `Atomics.wait(int32Array, index, value, timeout_ms)`. The wait suspends the worker until either timeout or another context calls `Atomics.notify` on the same word. This gives:
- Sub-millisecond sleep accuracy via timeout.
- Instant wake on slider/restart messages (main calls `Atomics.notify` after posting the message; worker wakes from `wait`, drains the message queue, resumes pacing).

Spec this in the mission. Acceptance criterion #2 ("sliding target TPS slider does not affect frame rate — only sim throughput") implicitly requires it: if pacing breaks above 250 TPS, the user will report "the slider doesn't go higher than ~250."

Note: main thread cannot `Atomics.wait` on `window`, but it can `Atomics.notify` freely; the message-passing direction (main → worker) goes through `postMessage` and the worker calls `Atomics.notify` to itself between message-drain and next tick. Workable. Just spec it.

## Important issues (should address before shipping)

### I1. The "Wasm crate code stays as-is" claim is wrong

**Source:** Mission §"What does NOT change", first bullet.

Path A in C1 requires a new Rust API (`write_snapshot_to(dst: js_sys::Uint8Array)` or equivalent). The grass and creature SoA writers need to learn how to memcpy into an externally-supplied byte slice instead of returning a `js_sys::Float32Array::view(&self.buf)`. Roughly:

- New `WorldHandle::write_snapshot(creatures_dst: &Uint8Array, grass_dst: &Uint8Array, stats_dst: &Uint8Array)`. Worker calls it once per snapshot.
- `creatures_buffer`, `grass_buffer_u8`, `creature_ids_buffer` stay for the legacy path (Stage 1 postMessage uses them; tests use them; the inspector ID resolution uses them).

This is a small surface delta but the mission shouldn't claim "stays as-is."

### I2. Inspector / slider / restart message protocols are unnamed

**Source:** Mission §"What does change" enumerates that these become messages; Stage 3 specs inspector click format only.

Recommend specifying as a small table in the mission. Fewer than 10 message types total. Something like:

| direction | name | shape | freq |
|---|---|---|---|
| main→worker | `set_slider` | `{name: string, value: number}` | per slider change |
| main→worker | `set_target_tps` | `{tps: number}` | per dropdown change |
| main→worker | `set_paused` | `{paused: bool}` | per spacebar |
| main→worker | `inspect_at` | `{wx, wy, tolerance, request_id}` | per click |
| main→worker | `inspect_id` | `{id, request_id}` | per inspector refresh frame |
| main→worker | `request_nn_stats` | `{request_id}` | 1 Hz |
| main→worker | `request_profile_report` | `{request_id}` | 1 Hz |
| worker→main | `ready` | `{snapshot_sab: SharedArrayBuffer, ...}` | once at boot/restart |
| worker→main | `inspect_reply` | `{request_id, json?: string}` | once per request |
| worker→main | `stats_reply` | `{request_id, json: string}` | once per request |

This is what an implementer will write anyway; documenting it surfaces the boot-handshake and the request_id correlation pattern, both of which determine restart-flow correctness.

### I3. Restart sequence has implicit ordering bugs

**Source:** Mission §"Risks + mitigations" Restart row; "terminate worker, spawn new one, re-init wasm + threadpool, then hand SAB pointers back to main. Brief (~100ms) blip."

Three issues:

1. **Rayon thread-pool re-init cost.** `wasm-bindgen-rayon::initThreadPool(navigator.hardwareConcurrency)` is not free — it spawns N nested workers, each loads the wasm module, sets up TLS. From the `[threads] pool ready` log timing in `e29c94d`, expect ~200–400 ms not ~100. Restart blip is closer to 0.5 s. Set expectations honestly.
2. **Old SAB handle on main.** Main holds typed-array views over the old worker's SAB. After `terminate()`, those views remain valid (the SAB is GC-rooted by the views). The renderer will keep painting the *last* tick of the *old* world until the new SAB pointers arrive. Acceptable, but explicit.
3. **Slider state carry-over.** Today `reapplyDevSliders(world)` runs after restart (`web/src/main.ts:176`) to re-push the user's tweaks. Post-decoupling this becomes a flurry of `set_slider` messages on the new worker. Spec when to send them: after `ready` arrives, before the first `set_paused(false)`. Otherwise the new worker runs default sliders for one tick.

### I4. The worker-stats and perf panels need a polling protocol

**Source:** `web/src/widgets/worker-stats.ts:104-153` (750 ms polling); `web/src/perf.ts` reads `profile_report_json()` from main.

Both of these run on the main thread today and read directly from the wasm instance. Post-decoupling:

- `nn_worker_stats_json` and `profile_report_json` move into the sim worker.
- Main poll loops post `request_nn_stats` / `request_profile_report` and render on reply.
- JSON staleness budget: 750 ms for nn stats (matches current panel), 1 s for profile report. The user does not notice ≤1 frame of stats lag.
- The TS-side `frame` subtree (`web/src/perf.ts`) stays main-side and is merged with the Rust `tick` subtree at render-time in the perf panel. That merge logic stays — only the data source for the Rust half is now a postMessage reply.

The mission's §"What does NOT change" bullet 3 says "Profiler architecture stays" — true at the topology level, but the data path moves. Worth one sentence.

### I5. Stage 1 postMessage payloads may *regress* current performance

**Source:** Mission Stage 1: "Slow (postMessage clones the payload — ~280 KB/tick is too much), but proves the architecture."

At pop=2000 + 60 TPS, structured-clone of 280 KB × 60 = 16.8 MB/s of clone work. Chrome's structured clone for typed arrays + plain objects is fast but not free: ~100–500 ns/KB depending on shape, putting Stage 1's clone cost at 1.6–8 ms/sec on main. Plus another ~8 ms/sec on the worker side. At pop=4000 these double.

Today's main-thread sim doesn't pay any cross-thread cost at all. Stage 1, in isolation, is **slower** than today on the dominant metric (frame rate at high pop). The mission frames Stage 1 as a checkpoint, but if the user runs it expecting a perf win they'll see a regression.

Two options: (a) acknowledge Stage 1 is a correctness checkpoint not a perf checkpoint; (b) skip postMessage and go straight to SAB (riskier; harder to debug). Recommend (a) — explicitly state in Stage 1's "Acceptance" that perf-vs-today comparison must wait for Stage 2.

### I6. `crossOriginIsolated` check happens on a single thread today; where does it move?

**Source:** `web/src/main.ts:92-109` logs SAB availability + isolation on main.

The post-decoupling check belongs in the sim worker (that's where SAB is consumed). But the user wants to see the log without opening a separate DevTools console pane — the current single-console UX is a small but real ergonomic win.

Recommendation: keep the check on main *and* in the worker. Main's check decides whether to spawn the sim worker as threaded vs single-threaded. Worker's check is a runtime safety net (re-verifies isolation; logs `[sim] rayon workers: N`). Two lines of redundant logging is cheap insurance against the v1.5 footgun.

### I7. Determinism: slider application order vs tick boundaries

**Source:** `World::step()` is deterministic given `(seed, sliders, tick_count)`. Today sliders mutate `DevSliders` between `step_n(N)` calls (`web/src/main.ts:235`); ordering is "all sliders set before step, then step batch."

Post-decoupling: a slider message arrives mid-batch. If the worker processes it inside `step_n(N)` (impossible — wasm-bindgen calls are atomic from JS's perspective), or right before/after — determinism is preserved iff the worker drains the message queue at a *fixed* point in the loop (recommend: between the inner tick's `writeSnapshot` and the `Atomics.wait`).

Spec this explicitly. Otherwise a future bug ("seed S diverged between runs") will be hard to trace.

### I8. Per-frame status DOM read is a wasm call today

**Source:** `web/src/main.ts:251-253` reads `world.tick`, `world.population`, `world.world_ended` for the status bar (5 Hz throttled).

These three getters move to worker round-trips. At 5 Hz the latency budget is 200 ms — easy. Recommend including `tick`, `pop`, `world_ended` in the snapshot stats block (already specced at <100 B/snapshot) so the status update is a free typed-array read instead of a request/reply.

## Lesser issues (worth knowing, not blocking)

- **Vite worker bundling.** Mission §"References" doesn't mention `web/vite.config.ts` worker config — already set to `format: "es"` for `wasm-bindgen-rayon`. The sim worker will inherit this. Confirm in Stage 1.
- **`new Worker(new URL(...), {type: 'module'})`** is the spawning pattern. Need to verify the wasm-bindgen-rayon worker pool (nested workers from the sim worker) works in Safari ≥15, Firefox ≥111. Modern; almost certainly fine; one-line check.
- **Hotkeys.** `r` for restart, `~` for settings, space for pause — all live on `window.addEventListener("keydown", ...)` on main. Stay on main; pause sends `set_paused`; restart triggers worker respawn. No spec change needed but worth a one-line note.
- **`reapplyDevSliders`** today reads 20+ slider getters and pushes them in one loop. Post-decoupling: this is a burst of 20 `set_slider` messages. Either batch into one `set_sliders_bulk` message or accept the burst (postMessage queue handles it fine).
- **`pack_render_buffer` is reused for the highlight ring pass** (`web/src/render-gl.ts:417`). Highlights read `world.creatures_buffer()` directly. Both call sites need to read from the SAB snapshot post-decoupling.
- **Tests.** `cargo test --lib` and `--features threads` flows don't run wasm-bindgen at all — Rust changes (new `write_snapshot_to` API) need native-only test paths (`#[cfg(target_arch = "wasm32")]` gates on the wasm exports; native tests against the helper).
- **The "sleep" estimate.** Mission's pseudocode at 60 TPS implies ~12 ms `Atomics.wait` per tick. That's 12 ms × N workers if the worker is the *only* thing keeping rayon's thread pool busy — fine; rayon workers are parked in `Atomics.wait` between tasks anyway.
- **Boot ordering claim "two wasm boot sequences."** Path A (recommended) has *one* wasm instance (sim worker only). Main has zero wasm. The mission's question is moot post-Path-A.
- **`render-gl.ts:330` stride guard hardcodes `stride !== 6`.** Already fine for v1.6 (no creature stride change in this mission). Just noting it'll need to match the snapshot stride post-decoupling.
- **Sim worker DevTools console.** Risk-table mitigation says "Errors land in a separate DevTools console panel ('Sim Worker')". True in Chrome (workers appear under "Sources / Threads"); also true in Firefox (under "Debugger / Workers"). Document in `docs/dev-server-prompt.md` as risk-table promises.
- **TypeScript types for the message protocol.** Recommend a `web/src/sim-bridge.ts` exports `SimMessage`/`SimReply` discriminated unions. Catches typos at type-check time.
- **The status bar's TPS readout.** Today the `tps` getter (`wasm_api.rs:146`) is a rolling 60-tick average. Post-decoupling it's worker-side; include in snapshot stats block at <100 B.

## Open questions for the user (consolidated)

### Architecture decisions (need answers before implementation)

1. **Path A or Path B for SAB / wasm ownership?** (C1) Recommend A.
2. **Frustum-cull location post-decoupling — worker, main, or JS-on-SAB?** (C2) Recommend "main does cull on JS-side SAB read."
3. **Snapshot stride — keep at 24 B/creature (mission as-written) or grow to 32 B for id?** (C3) Recommend 32 B.
4. **Pacing primitive — `Atomics.wait`, `setTimeout`, or some hybrid?** (C5) Recommend `Atomics.wait`.

### Behavior decisions

5. **Stage 1 acceptance criterion — "TPS independent of FPS at correctness level" vs "FPS not worse than today"?** (I5) Recommend the former.
6. **Inspector subscription streaming — non-goal as written, or fold into Stage 3 if there's time?** (Lesser, but it's the difference between a click-to-info-in-2-frames experience and a click-to-info-in-1-frame experience.)
7. **Slider-drain ordering — between snapshot-write and Atomics.wait, or at top of next loop iteration?** (I7) Recommend between snapshot-write and wait so the slider takes effect for the *next* tick deterministically.

## Final word

The mission has the right instinct and the right shape, and the v1.5 substrate genuinely makes it feasible. What it lacks is the discipline its own v1.5 predecessor got reviewed into: **decide the load-bearing design choices in writing**, **do the arithmetic on the budget claims**, and **say what the implementer will actually type**. The five Critical issues above are not deep architectural problems — they're "the mission is two sentences shy of being a spec." Fold the C1–C5 + I1–I8 edits in and this ships clean at 2.5–3 days with frame rate genuinely decoupled from sim throughput at every TPS in the dropdown.

The single highest-leverage edit: **commit to Path A in writing and add the snapshot id column.** Everything else falls out.
