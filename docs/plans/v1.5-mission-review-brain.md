# v1.5 mission review — brain design (R1)

## TL;DR

The 32-input redesign is a real upgrade — semantic, positive-signal inputs over 8-sector aggregates is the right direction and a meaningful improvement over the 72-RGB-slot bandwidth dump. **The single biggest concern is the curriculum subsystem**: smoothstep-gated upkeep that goes to **zero below `min_pop=1000`** doesn't just smear selection pressure, it **deletes the fitness gradient that the brain redesign exists to reward**. You will ship the most legible brain you've ever had into a world where, for the first 1000 creatures, no decision the brain makes affects survival. That is not a curriculum — it is an anaesthetic. The single best call is removing the color block from vision and replacing the NN-hash with action-EMA. Verdict: ship the brain + vision + color changes; cut or radically rework curriculum; treat the "He-init" math in §"Implementation order" as a number that needs to be derived, not asserted.

## What the mission gets right

- **Removing the 72-slot RGB vision block.** Color-as-vision was always a confounder — creatures could be selected on "see red things" without "red things" carrying any consistent semantic. Replacing with `creature_proximity × 8` is the right move. See mission §"Brain inputs / Vision (21)".
- **Positive-signal convention** (`hunger` not `energy`, `cooldown_ready` not `cooldown_frac`). Under ReLU/LReLU with small init, a 1-valued signal contributes meaningfully through its row weights, while a 0-valued signal contributes nothing. The brain spends fewer weight perturbations learning "subtract from baseline."
- **`curr_grass_density` as a separate slot.** Distinguishes "should I move toward grass" (sector signal) from "should I bite NOW" (under-foot signal). Two genuinely different decisions; deserves two slots.
- **`cooldown_ready` over `cooldown_frac`.** Step-like signals at meaningful decision boundaries are easier for a ReLU stack to gate on than a smoothly-rising fraction.
- **Keeping `tanh` on velocity heads, nothing on action logits.** Velocity must be bounded; argmax doesn't care about scale or sign.
- **Coincidental weight-count parity (2808).** Keeps the mutation defaults `(rate=0.02, σ=0.02)` in the same ballpark as v1.3 so you're not changing the brain and the evolutionary search dynamics in the same patch. Worth calling out explicitly in §"Brain architecture".
- **Abandoning weight-hash colors for action-EMA.** The single most user-facing improvement in the mission. Color now answers "what is this creature doing?" instead of "what's its genome fingerprint?" — and the strobe-on-mutation problem goes away.
- **Differentiated EMA timescales per channel** (Graze ±0.01/0.01, Split +0.5/-0.005, Red +0.1/-0.001). Action frequencies differ by 1–2 orders of magnitude; a single global rate would saturate the common channel and undersignal the rare one. Per-channel asymmetric deltas are correct.
- **Architecture-future notes.** "Try wider before deeper" and "no inverse pyramid" are both correct prescriptions for a small-NN gradient-free optimizer.
- **Out-of-scope discipline.** Explicitly forbidding NEAT, topology evolution, and "stable equilibrium as success criterion" keeps the mission shippable.

## Concerns — high (likely to bite)

### 1. Curriculum subsystem deletes the selection gradient at the exact time the brain needs it

**What the mission says** (§"Curriculum"):
> Below `min_pop`: factor = 0. Free upkeep / moves / eats. Only old age kills.
> Above `max_pop`: factor = 1. Full slider values apply.
> Intentional behavior: if pop collapses below `min_pop`, costs back off to zero so the population can recover. User-confirmed acceptable.

**Why this will bite:**

- The "evolve to higher peaks" goal assumes a fitness gradient exists. With `factor = 0` below 1000, the *only* selection pressure is `max_age` (5000 ticks). Movement is free. Eating is free. Splitting takes 50 energy and then you get it back from nothing. **The brain weights are functionally invisible to evolution in this regime** — every random brain reaches `max_age` and splits some number of times before dying of old age.
- This is *worse* than v1.3, not better. v1.3 had constant moderate pressure; v1.5 says "no pressure until we're crowded." Founder lineages don't get filtered — they all survive equally well. You'll see lineage diversity stay artificially high for hours of wall time, then *crash simultaneously* when the population finally crosses 1000.
- The "if pop collapses below min_pop, costs back off" rule is a homeostatic feedback loop that will produce **oscillation, not equilibrium.** Pop crosses 1000 → costs ramp → marginal lineages die → pop drops below 1000 → costs vanish → everyone breeds → ... This is classic Lotka-Volterra-with-overshoot dynamics; it does not converge.
- Worse: it actively rewards "be lazy below threshold." A lineage whose brain happens to be terrible at finding grass *can persist indefinitely* below 1000.
- The smoothstep ramp doesn't fix this; it just spreads the cliff across a band. Below `min_pop` the selection signal is still zero.

**Concrete recommendation:**

Replace "factor goes to zero" with **"factor has a floor."** Concretely:
- `factor = MIN_FACTOR + (1.0 - MIN_FACTOR) * smoothstep(min_pop, max_pop, current_pop)` with `MIN_FACTOR = 0.25` or so.
- This keeps a *real* gradient at all population levels — bad brains still die at low pop, just more slowly — while still relieving pressure when the population is fragile.

If the user insists on `factor = 0` below threshold, the brain redesign is **largely wasted effort** during the regime where most learning would otherwise happen. Be explicit about that tradeoff in the mission.

### 2. "He-init regime" claim in §"Implementation order" is hand-wavy

**What the mission says** (§"Implementation order", step 5):
> Bump `NN_INIT_RANGE` 0.3 → ~0.4 for the He-init regime.

**Why this is wrong as written:**

He initialization (Kaiming, ReLU-appropriate) prescribes `Var(W) = 2 / fan_in`, which for uniform `U(-r, +r)` gives `r = sqrt(6 / fan_in)`.

- Layer 1: `fan_in = 32` → `r_He = sqrt(6/32) ≈ 0.433`. ✓ — `0.4` is close.
- Layer 2: `fan_in = 48` → `r_He = sqrt(6/48) ≈ 0.354`.
- Layer 3 (output): `fan_in = 24` → `r_He = sqrt(6/24) = 0.5`.

A single global `NN_INIT_RANGE = 0.4` is **too small for layer 3 outputs** (50% under-scale) and **too large for layer 2** (12% over). Output logits will be underscaled at init, making early decisions weak/noisy and Graze-dominant (since first-index tiebreak on near-zero logits returns Graze).

Worse: under mutation σ=0.02 the population mean drifts. After ~100 generations the effective scale isn't 0.4 anymore — it's set by `Var ≈ σ² × n_mutations`. He-init only matters at *founder* time.

**Concrete recommendation:**
- Either implement *per-layer* init ranges (`r_l1 = 0.43, r_l2 = 0.35, r_l3 = 0.5`) — costs 3 constants and ~6 LOC — OR
- Pick a defensible single value: I'd argue `0.5` over `0.4`, because under-scaled output logits at founder time bias action selection toward "fallback Graze."
- Don't write "He-init regime" in the mission unless the math is shown.

### 3. Leaky ReLU + uniform-random mutation: claim that it "avoids dying neurons" needs evidence

**What the mission says** (§"Brain architecture"):
> Leaky ReLU in hidden layers (avoids dying-neuron failure mode of plain ReLU under random weight perturbation; same cost as ReLU).

**Why this might fail:**

- "Dying ReLU" is a *training* pathology — gradient descent pushes a unit's bias permanently negative; gradient stays zero forever. In *gradient-free evolution*, there is no gradient to vanish. The whole concept doesn't cleanly apply.
- What *does* apply: L-ReLU preserves sign information, which means a mutation that flips the sign of a hidden unit's output isn't binary (live↔dead) but gradual. This makes the fitness landscape *smoother* under random perturbation. **This is the right argument, not "dying neurons."**
- The mutation kernel adds `normal() * 0.02` to weights in `[-0.4, 0.4]` — ~5% perturbation. With ~281 mutations per child, the cumulative drift dominates L-ReLU's 0.01 leak slope. Don't expect a *measurable* difference vs plain ReLU.

**Concrete recommendation:**
- Ship L-ReLU — cost is zero — but rewrite the justification as: "preserves sign information so single-weight mutations produce smoother fitness changes."
- Don't dismiss **`tanh` in hidden layers** out of hand. It's defensible in this regime (small NN, gradient-free, bounded inputs).

### 4. 8-sector aliasing is undersold as a hazard

**What the mission says** (§"Brain inputs"):
> `creature_proximity × 8` ... sector aliasing accepted.

**Why this might fail:**

- 8 sectors = 45° each. At distance 20u, a 45° sector has an arc length of ~15.7u. A creature at sector boundary jitter will *flip between adjacent sectors every few ticks* as the angle drifts.
- Hidden units that learn to gate on "creature in sector 3" will misfire on neighbors at the 22.5° boundary.
- The spec doesn't say whether sector 0 is world-east or creature-forward. Creatures have no heading state today — only vx/vy. So sectors are necessarily world-aligned. **This is the right choice** but the mission should state it.
- 24-sector v1.3 vision had 15°/sector. Going to 8 is a 3× resolution reduction — a *lot* for a system whose only spatial signal is sector-based.

**Concrete recommendation:**
- **Explicitly document** that sectors are world-aligned (N/E/S/W and the 4 diagonals).
- Consider **bilinear sector weighting**: a creature at angle 50° contributes 0.78 to sector 1 (45°) and 0.22 to sector 2 (90°). Eliminates flicker at sector boundaries; same data size; modest extra cost.

### 5. "Plateau" hypothesis is unsupported

**What the mission says** (§"Why now"):
> The current 112-input brain plateaus quickly. The 72-slot RGB vision and 25-slot grass-patch blocks are bandwidth without semantic value, and a single hidden layer with `tanh` can't hierarchically compose features.

**Why this is suspect:**

- "Plateaus quickly" is asserted, not measured. There are no fitness logs, no per-lineage performance metrics, no behavioral diversity metrics in the repo.
- Equally plausible alternative explanations:
  - **Founder bias.** Single-founder seeding; 80% of runs go extinct in 100–400 ticks per v1.4-directions §1. That's "the run died before the brain could do anything," not "the brain plateaued."
  - **Mutation rate untuned.** `NN_MUT_SIGMA_DEFAULT = 0.02` and `NN_MUT_RATE_DEFAULT = 0.02` inherited unchanged from v6, never tuned. v1.4 directions §E4 called this out.
  - **Selection pressure too weak.** Energy-only fitness signal is bimodal (you either die at 0 or survive forever) — no gradient between "fit" and "very fit." That's an ecology problem, not architecture.
  - **Action-space ceiling.** Even a perfect brain has only 3 strategy options modulated by 2 velocity components.
- The 72-slot RGB vision is genuinely low-value. But the *25-slot grass-patch* is high-quality local information; calling it "bandwidth without semantic value" is wrong — and the mission then re-introduces almost-equivalent information via `grass_density × 8` + `curr_grass_density`.
- A 2-hidden-layer 48→24 net has ~2800 weights, same as v1.3. **You're betting that *where* you spend the weights matters more than how many you have.** Plausible (I lean toward agreeing) — but frame as hypothesis, not obvious win.

**Concrete recommendation:**
- Soften §"Why now" language — "we *believe* the 112-input brain has architectural ceilings we want to explore around."
- Add a quantitative success metric: e.g., mean-creature-age over a 10k-tick window, or action-frequency-variance. Otherwise "the brain is better" is unfalsifiable.
- **Multi-founder seeding (v1.4 E1) is more likely to remove the "evolution stalls" problem than the brain redesign is.** XS effort. The mission doesn't mention it — that's a real gap.

### 6. Removing all 24-sector RGB vision loses inter-creature recognition entirely

**What the mission says** (§"Brain inputs"):
> Color block removed entirely. Vision becomes about *where* things are, not *what color* they are.

**Why this might bite:**

- Once color-EMA reflects strategy (Red = Eat, Green = Graze, Blue = Split), color is genuinely informative. But the brain *cannot see it* — only the user can. Color is now pure-UX, not a selection target.
- You've shipped a world where eating others is a strategy with no way for prey to identify a predator before contact. The 8-sector creature_proximity signal says "something is close" but not "the close thing is a known eater."
- Any "smart prey avoidance" strategy is unreachable. Selection on eat-resistance reduces to "outrun anything that approaches."

**Concrete recommendation:**
- Either accept this and document: "creatures cannot perceive each other's color; prey-recognition is v1.6+." OR
- Add a single `nearest_creature_is_red` signal (1 float) — the dominant EMA channel of the *nearest* creature within range. Cheap (~5 LOC since you're already finding nearest-creature for sector aggregation), slots into one of the 2 padding slots. Minimum viable predator-signaling input.

## Concerns — medium

### 7. `prev_vx, prev_vy` is half a memory system

v1.3 had `last_action` *and* `last2_action` plus `prev_energy` (energy delta). The mission drops the 2-tick depth and the energy-delta signal — the latter being one of the few inputs that directly answers "is my strategy working right now."

Without energy-delta, the brain cannot directly distinguish "I'm grazing successfully" from "I'm grazing on empty cells." It has to infer this from `curr_grass_density` over time, requiring temporal integration the architecture doesn't provide.

**Recommendation:** Replace one of the 2 padding slots with `energy_delta_clamped`. Or replace `is_last_action_eat` (low information — Eat is rare and gated) with the energy delta.

### 8. `ticks_since_last_split` clamp is arbitrary

`SPLIT_THRESHOLD = 50` energy and at base upkeep ~0.17/tick, a creature recovers from a split in ~294 ticks — so 100 saturates well before the next viable split. The signal will read 1.0 for most of a creature's life. Not useless ("just split" refractory indicator) but the scale is mismatched.

**Recommendation:** Either tie the divisor to `SPLIT_THRESHOLD / UPKEEP_BASE`, or document that this is intentionally a 100-tick refractory signal regardless of split economics.

### 9. Action-EMA decay rates don't account for argmax-fallthrough

Graze is the **fallback action** (per `decode_action` in `src/world/nn.rs:264-299`) when Eat or Split is gated. A creature can argmax-want-Eat-but-be-in-cooldown and get scored as a Grazer. Over the 50-tick digestion cooldown that's 50 "false Graze" ticks pushing green up.

A successful predator with Eat as primary will spend ~50% of its time in cooldown showing Graze. Its color becomes Green + Red, not Red. User sees a "mixed" creature that's actually a specialist Eater.

**Recommendation:** Score color by **argmax output before validity fallthrough**, not the final action. ~5 LOC.

### 10. Color encoding has channel collision

Green=Graze, Blue=Split, Red=bite. No representation for "this creature does nothing useful" — its color will be black (all channels decay toward 0). Black on typical render background is hard to read.

**Recommendation:** Floor each channel at 0.15. Or set the *display* color to `max(rgb, 0.15)`.

### 11. Worker utilization widget needs per-pass breakdown

With 3 parallel passes (NN forward, vision, grass-sector), aggregate active count is misleading — if NN is at 16/16 but vision is at 8/16 you want to know that. Per-pass breakdown.

## Concerns — low (worth noting, not blocking)

### 12. `1.0` constant input slot is a vestige
The "implicit bias" trick is fine but should be acknowledged in code — this is the *only* way the no-bias architecture learns biases. Comment that this slot is load-bearing.

### 13. Wall proximity range (50u) is asymmetric with creature/grass (20u)
Fine — mission justifies it — but means the brain's "what's near" perceptual scale is non-uniform. A wall at 30u reads 0.4; a creature at 30u reads 0. Learnable, but harder than uniform scaling.

### 14. SIMD padding rationale is right but counter to "32 = clean number"
`NN_INPUTS = 32 = 4 × 8` is the right SIMD shape. Reserving padding for "future inputs" means you'll break weight compatibility *again* when you use them. Better to commit to using them now or formally drop to 24 (3 × 8).

### 15. `is_last_action_eat` is low-info
Eat fires rarely (gated by cooldown + proximity). Signal will be 0 for ~95% of ticks. Two slots (graze + eat one-hots) carry less information than a single `energy_delta` slot would.

### 16. Larger init range interacts with mutation σ
Bigger weights are more sensitive to small mutations in relative terms. Either test σ default at the new init range, or bump σ proportionally. Don't change init range *and* architecture *and* inputs in the same commit without a fitness measurement.

## Things the mission doesn't address that it should

- **No multi-founder seeding.** v1.4-directions §E1 identified this as XS-effort, L-impact to fix the 80% extinction problem. Without it, every run starts with one random brain that may be unable to find grass — and curriculum's free-upkeep regime will let even non-functional founders persist, hiding the issue. **Multi-founder + curriculum is *especially* bad**: 8 random founders + free upkeep → 8 useless lineages persist forever below 1000 pop. Add E1 (founder_count slider, default 8) to this mission.
- **No fitness metric.** Success criterion #2 ("color visibly reflects strategy") is qualitative. Add quantitative: e.g., "mean creature age > 500 by tick 10000" or "Eat-action frequency > 0.01 by tick 5000."
- **Mutation parameter re-tuning.** Step 5 changes init range but not `NN_MUT_RATE_DEFAULT` or `NN_MUT_SIGMA_DEFAULT`. With different input scale + architecture, optimal mutation rates almost certainly differ. At minimum flag this as an unchanged-by-design decision.
- **No mention of `prev_energy` SoA field.** v1.3 uses `creatures.prev_energy[i]` for energy-delta. The mission removes the energy-delta input but doesn't say what happens to the underlying field. If it stays, you have dead state.
- **Color is per-creature, not per-lineage.** Two siblings diverge in color the moment one eats and the other doesn't. Correct for action-EMA, but means *the user cannot identify a "lineage" by color*. v1.4 E2 (lineage tag) is the natural complement — mention or explicitly defer.
- **No spec for `is_last_action_split` (third action).** "Split implied when both = 0" is a clever 2-bit encoding for 3 states, but means a brain that wants "I just split" has to compute `1 - graze - eat` from two AND-like operations. Recommend explicit 3-way one-hot if there's space.
- **No interaction note about color-EMA and reproduction.** Does a newborn inherit parent's color-EMA state, or start at zero? Specify.
- **The Eat success delta (+0.1) is event-driven; how is it computed?** Need a per-creature "bites this tick" counter. `tick.rs` already partially tracks this via `scratch_got_a_bite`. State this is the data source.

## Things that should be cut from scope

- **Curriculum subsystem, as currently designed.** See concern #1. Either rework with `MIN_FACTOR` floor, or cut entirely. If it's opt-in and defaulted off, the mission text overweights its importance. If defaulted on, it will silently sabotage the brain redesign's evaluability.
- **"Promote color deltas to live sliders later if iteration demands it"** — leave as code comment but don't appear in the implementation order. Hardcoded values are correct for v1.5; avoid premature configurability.
- **The "research pointers" in §"Architecture / topology notes"** (Universal approximation, NEAT, MAP-Elites, CMA-ES). Belong in a separate brainstorm doc. They're aspirational and risk being read as commitments.

## Recommended changes (numbered, prioritized)

1. **Rework curriculum to floor-not-zero.** `factor = MIN_FACTOR + (1 - MIN_FACTOR) * smoothstep(...)`. Default `MIN_FACTOR = 0.25`. OR cut curriculum entirely. (Highest-impact change.)
2. **Fix the He-init math.** Per-layer init ranges (`0.43, 0.35, 0.5`) or pick `0.5` as a single value and acknowledge the layer-1 over-init. Show derivation.
3. **Add multi-founder seeding to this mission.** XS effort; defends against the failure mode that will *especially* mask brain-redesign efficacy. Default 8.
4. **Reintroduce `energy_delta` as a brain input.** Use one of the 2 padding slots. Cheapest input-tier change restoring a critical in-life fitness signal.
5. **Specify color-EMA inheritance at split.** Pick "child starts at parent's color" or "child starts at zero." Document.
6. **Document sector orientation (world-aligned vs creature-relative).** Single sentence; prevents implementer drift.
7. **Soften the "plateau" language.** Replace assertion with hypothesis; add at least one quantitative success-criterion metric.
8. **Score color by raw-argmax, not validated-action.** Avoids predator-in-cooldown reading as Grazer. ~5 LOC.
9. **Floor display color at 0.15 per channel.**
10. **Reframe L-ReLU justification** from "avoids dying neurons" to "preserves sign information under random perturbation."
11. **Bilinear sector weighting** for `creature_proximity × 8`. Eliminates boundary flicker.
12. **Add `nearest_creature_dominant_color` input** (1 slot, into padding) so prey can perceive predator intent. Cheap; otherwise color-EMA is pure UX.

## Open questions for the user

1. **Do you want curriculum to actually anaesthetize selection at low pop, or to soften it?** If anaesthetize, how do you plan to evaluate whether the new brain is better than the old one?
2. **Is "the brain plateaus quickly" a measurement or a vibe?** If a measurement, what's the metric? If a vibe, add an objective metric *before* the redesign so you can compare.
3. **Should color-EMA persist across reproduction?** Does a newborn inherit parent's EMA state, or start at zero?
4. **Should the brain be able to perceive other creatures' colors?** Mission removes vision-by-color but action-EMA makes color meaningful — deliberate choice or oversight?
5. **Is the 32-input shape locked, or open to 32 → 24** by dropping padding + low-info slots? 24 = 3×8 is still SIMD-aligned. Smaller-faster brain.
6. **Are you committed to population-feedback curriculum?** Or open to *time-based* (costs ramp 0→1 over first 5000 ticks, then stay at 1)? Time-based avoids oscillation entirely.
7. **Multi-founder: in-scope or not?**
8. **Should `NN_MUT_SIGMA_DEFAULT` / `NN_MUT_RATE_DEFAULT` change with the new architecture?** Currently the mission changes everything *except* mutation parameters.
