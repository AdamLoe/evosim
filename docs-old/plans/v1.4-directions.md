# v1.4 Directions

Baseline: main @ `a1bd1bf` (post-v1.3 simplification). Net codebase ≈ 9.5k LOC. Sim is "pure NN evolution" — all creatures structurally identical, 112-input × 24-hidden × 5-output brain (2808 weights), grass-only food, walled world, 24-sector RGB vision, color from `xxhash64(weights)`. TPS ≈ 8000+ at default sliders; ~80% of seeds extinct within 100–400 ticks.

This document picks **3–5 concrete proposals per category** with effort × impact sizing and a `Recommended v1.4 shape` shortlist at the end.

Convention used below for sizing:
- **Effort**: XS (≤ ½ day) / S (1 day) / M (2–4 days) / L (1 week) / XL (≥ 2 weeks).
- **Impact**: XS (cosmetic) / S (nice-to-have) / M (visibly better) / L (changes how the project feels) / XL (unlocks a new class of behavior).

---

## 1. Performance opportunities

The v1.3-M3 plan deferred most backend perf to v1.4. The shape is now stable enough to land them without re-doing measurements every commit. We also have a smaller NN (30 % fewer weights), uniform creature shape, and only one render-path tint (grass).

### P1. Brain weight transpose for `forward()`

**What:** Reshape `Brain::weights` so the input→hidden matmul iterates over `input` in the outer loop and accumulates a `[NN_HIDDEN]` vector in lockstep, instead of one hidden unit per outer iteration.

**Why now:** Identified in v1.2 perf-hot-loop #1/#18/#20; deferred explicitly in `v1.3-M3-perf.md` §4.1. With v1.3's smaller weight count (2808 vs 4032) and identical-shape creatures, the matmul is the single largest CPU cost per tick — `Brain::forward` runs once per creature per tick on a population of 500–2000.

**Effort:** M. Two layers (input→hidden, hidden→output), one hash-stable rewrite of `Brain::founder` / `child_from` to keep `weight_hash` semantics (since hashing happens over the raw `Vec<f32>`, the byte order matters for `color_rgb` reproducibility). Either transpose only the in-memory representation while keeping the canonical hashed order, or accept a one-time color reshuffle.

**Impact:** L. The forward pass is the top-3 hot loop. Realistic win 25–50 % on `tick.nn_forward` span, which currently dominates the per-tick budget at heavy pop.

**Sketch:** Add a `weights_transposed: Vec<f32>` cache rebuilt by `Brain::child_from`. `forward()` iterates `i in 0..NN_INPUTS { let xi = input[i]; for h_chunk in 0..HIDDEN_CHUNKS { acc[h] += xi * w_t[i, h]; } }` then ReLU + layer 2. Keep `weights` as the canonical (hash) representation; `weights_transposed` is derived.

**Risks/uncertainties:** memory doubles per brain (2808 × 4 bytes × 2 ≈ 22 kB per creature → 22 MB at pop=1000). Test that the doubled allocation isn't worse than the matmul win.

### P2. `GrassGrid::step` SIMD via `wide::f32x8`

**What:** Vectorize the inner ix-loop of `step()` using `f32x8`, reading `d[c]`, north/south/east/west neighbors as f32x8 chunks, applying logistic + propagation in SIMD.

**Why now:** v1.3 M1 already cut the cost 4× by going 240→120 grid dim. The function is now well-factored (`d`, `s`, clean ghost-zero boundary) and the per-cell work is straightforward.

**Effort:** S. `grass.rs` is small (~220 LOC of impl) and well-tested (15 unit tests). Border cells handled scalar; interior `[1..dim-1]` runs SIMD.

**Impact:** M. After M1, grass step is a few percent of tick cost — not the bottleneck, but free perf. More importantly: lays groundwork for letting users push `GRASS_GRID_DIM` higher (240 with SIMD ≈ today's 120 scalar).

**Sketch:** Split into `step_interior_simd` + `step_borders_scalar`. Inner loop: load 8 cells of d, 8 of d[c-1], 8 of d[c+1], shift for n/s. Replace logistic and clamp with `f32x8` ops.

**Risks/uncertainties:** correctness vs scalar — keep existing tests; add an SIMD-vs-scalar bit-equal test like `forward_pass_matches_scalar_reference` in brain.rs.

### P3. Render-path span instrumentation + Canvas2D batch fills

**What:** (a) Add per-call profiler spans in `web/src/perf.ts` wrapping `drawGrassMap` and `drawCreatures`. (b) Batch creature draws into a single `beginPath`/`fill` per color bucket as proposed in M3 §3.1.

**Why now:** v1.3 M5 made `color_rgb` a discrete u32 — a perfect bucket key. Today each creature triggers a fresh `beginPath` + `fillStyle` + ring strokes (rings always render even though all flags are constant `1` post-D3 — wasted work).

**Effort:** S. The bucketing rewrite is ~30 LOC in `render.ts`. Span wrapping is ~10 LOC.

**Impact:** M. M3 estimated 2–4 ms/frame saved at heavy load. Spans are a permanent diagnostic enabler — every future render work hinges on having them.

**Sketch:** `const buckets = new Map<number, number[]>(); for i…buckets.get(rgb).push(i);` then `for (const [color, idxs] of buckets) { ctx.fillStyle=…; ctx.beginPath(); for…ctx.arc(…); ctx.fill(); }`. Drop the 5 ring-draw calls entirely (the ring flags are all `1.0` constants post-D3 — visual delta is "rings always drawn" → "rings never drawn"; could optionally keep just the highlight ring).

**Risks/uncertainties:** rings are decorative; visual delta acceptable. Highlight ring stays per-creature.

### P4. Skip-aware `creatures_buffer()` rebuild

**What:** Maintain a frame-tag in `WorldHandle`; only refill `creature_buf` on ticks where positions/colors actually changed (i.e. always during play, but skip during pause).

**Why now:** `creatures_buffer` repacks 11 floats × N every frame even when speed=0 (paused). It also rebuilds during 60 Hz RAF while the sim is at speed=1 (only 30 ticks/s of actual change) — a 2× redundancy.

**Effort:** XS. Add `last_pack_tick: u32` + early-return when tick + slider state unchanged.

**Impact:** S. Mostly saves work during pause and at low sim speeds. At speed=100 the work is unchanged. Useful but minor.

**Sketch:** `if self.last_pack_tick == self.inner.tick { return existing view; }`. Bump on every fill.

**Risks/uncertainties:** sneaky — slider changes that affect render don't bump `tick`. Stick to position/color invariants.

### P5. Vision raycast tightening

**What:** Hoist sector trig out of the per-creature loop (already cached in `eye_trig`, but the per-tick read pattern could prefetch); collapse the wall-sentinel exit-bookkeeping; eliminate the `cells_walked >= MAX_CELLS_PER_RAY` guard (DDA inside walled 120×120 grid is bounded geometrically).

**Why now:** With D3 making all creatures use 24 sectors at `VISION_RANGE_MAX = 80`, the raycast inner loop is fully uniform. The wall-sentinel exit code (4 branches per ray exit) was added piecewise and can be simplified now that walls are the only world shape.

**Effort:** S. Read-only on `vision.rs` raycast logic; no behavioral change.

**Impact:** S. Vision is ~10–15 % of tick budget at heavy pop; trim ~20 % of that = ~2–3 % total tick win.

**Sketch:** Profile-first; if vision span > 1 ms at pop=1500, do it. Replace the two wall-sentinel arms with a single `RayHit::Wall(t)` returned at any out-of-bounds transition.

**Risks/uncertainties:** wall sentinel must remain visible to NN as gray RGB — easy to verify with existing `raycast_returns_wall_sentinel_at_world_edge` test.

## 2. Evolution improvements

The codebase is now stripped to "pure NN evolution." `~80 %` of seeds extinct within 100–400 ticks. The remaining seeds drift slowly because the only selection signal is `energy > SPLIT_THRESHOLD`.

### E1. Multi-founder seeding

**What:** Spawn N independently-randomized founders (default N=8–16, slider-controlled) instead of 1.

**Why now:** Already mentioned as a D8 follow-up. Single-founder extinction means a single bad weight draw kills the entire run; multi-founder ensures the user sees evolution within the first 30 seconds in most cases. Doesn't reintroduce F.30 hardwiring.

**Effort:** XS. `World::new` already pushes 1 creature; loop the push N times with different RNG-derived sub-seeds.

**Impact:** L. Drastically improves "open a tab, see evolution" UX. The 80 % extinction rate effectively becomes (0.8)^N — at N=8 that's < 17 %.

**Sketch:** Add `FOUNDER_COUNT_DEFAULT = 8` constant; dev slider `founder_count: u32` 1..32. Spread founders uniformly across the world or sample positions from a 2D halton sequence.

**Risks/uncertainties:** N founders compete for the same grass; if N is high and grass density is low, all N starve faster than one would. Tune in concert with grass sliders.

### E2. Lineage tag (lightweight, not species)

**What:** Add a `u32 lineage_id` to `CreatureSoA`. Founder N gets `lineage_id = N`. Children inherit parent's id (no anchor-weight clones, no threshold-based speciation). Purely for visualization.

**Why now:** Color hash drifts continuously with mutation; lineage tags give the user a discrete grouping. Helps the population-over-time and inspector panels distinguish "this is the founder-3 line" vs "founder-7 line." Sidesteps D10's deletion (no SpeciesRegistry, no anchor weights, no thresholding — just a u32 inherited from parent at birth).

**Effort:** S. Single column add; populate at `handle_births`. Render path optional (an outer ring colored by lineage hash, or just a panel showing per-lineage population).

**Impact:** M. Makes evolution legible without the cost of the deleted SpeciesRegistry. Unlocks "lineage extinction" chart.

**Sketch:** `lineage_id: Vec<u32>` next to `id`. Constructor sets `lineage_id[i] = i as u32`. `handle_births` copies parent's lineage. New wasm getter `lineage_counts_json()` returns `Map<u32, u32>` for the UI.

**Risks/uncertainties:** at high N and many splits, lineage labels are stable but uninformative (no fitness signal). Document as "ancestry tag, not species."

### E3. Crossover via sexual reproduction option

**What:** Add a `SEX_RATE` slider (default 0.0 = current asexual). When > 0, on a Split action, the parent occasionally requests a mate — a second nearby creature whose weights are averaged (with mutation on top) to form the child.

**Why now:** All current reproduction is mutation-only. Crossover is the single biggest evolutionary lever not yet explored, and the small NN (2808 weights) makes it cheap to implement (no NEAT, no topology surgery).

**Effort:** M. Need a mate-finder (grid scan for nearest creature with energy > some floor), a crossover operator (uniform per-weight average, or one-point bisection), and a new action variant? Or keep Split as the sole reproductive action and add `crossover_rate` slider that controls how often Split tries to find a mate first.

**Impact:** L. Sexual reproduction is the classic evolutionary primitive that hasn't been tried here. Potential for much faster fitness convergence.

**Sketch:** When `Action::Split` fires: roll `rng.uniform() < SEX_RATE`; if yes, find nearest creature within 2× body radius with `energy ≥ SPLIT_THRESHOLD/2`; create child weights = `0.5 * (self.weights + mate.weights)` then mutate; both parents pay split cost. Otherwise: current asexual path.

**Risks/uncertainties:** mate-finding adds a grid query per Split — manageable. Co-mate energy charging is a design question (does the mate consent / get charged?); v6 §K silent. Default OFF preserves baseline.

### E4. Mutation-rate tuning study (with new dev knobs)

**What:** Already have `nn_mutation_sigma` and `mutation_rate_multiplier` sliders. Add `nn_mutation_rate` direct override and a `mut_rate_jitter` slider. Document a 4-cell parameter sweep workflow.

**Why now:** Defaults (`NN_MUT_RATE_DEFAULT = 0.02`, `NN_MUT_SIGMA_DEFAULT = 0.02`) are inherited from v6 §E without empirical justification post-simplification. The smaller 2808-weight brain may want different rates than the v1.2 4032-weight brain.

**Effort:** XS for the sliders; S for a documented sweep workflow.

**Impact:** M. Even without code change, exposing the right dials lets users discover what works. Combined with E1 (multi-founder), creates a tunable evolution test bed.

**Sketch:** Add `set_nn_mutation_rate(v: f32)` wasm setter. Dev panel row: "nn mut rate" 0.0–0.5. Add a `MUT_RATE_OF_RATES` slider too. Write `docs/research/v1.4-mutation-sweep.md` with a recipe.

**Risks/uncertainties:** none — pure exposure of existing constants.

### E5. Boltzmann-style selection pressure via energy taxation

**What:** Optional `selection_temperature` slider that adds energy noise (creature `i` pays `energy * normal(0, T)` each tick). Hotter T = more genetic drift; colder T = stricter fitness.

**Why now:** Current selection is brittle (`energy <= 0 → death`). A continuous selection temperature lets the user dial the evolutionary pressure between "almost-neutral drift" and "ruthless selection." Cheap, reversible, no new structures.

**Effort:** S. One slider, one term in `energy_bookkeeping`.

**Impact:** M. Probably L for the kinds of experiments that become possible.

**Sketch:** `let noise = rng.normal() * T * energy; energy -= noise.max(-energy * 0.5);` in `energy_bookkeeping`. Default T=0 → no change.

**Risks/uncertainties:** uses RNG inside energy bookkeeping — currently deterministic. Make sure the RNG draw happens from `SimRng` so determinism survives. At T=0 (default), no RNG calls, no behavior change.

## 3. UI improvements

The inspector currently shows NN weight count + color hash + energy + a few constants. Lots of headroom.

### U1. Inspector "rich panel" with recent action histogram

**What:** Add to the inspector: (a) a 200-tick rolling histogram of `last_action` (3 bars: Graze/Eat/Split), (b) recent energy delta sparkline, (c) vision-ray hit ratio (% of 24 rays that hit something other than zero), (d) distance traveled, age, mutation rate (already exposed but not shown).

**Why now:** Per-creature NN behavior is opaque; the only feedback is "creature moves around." A small per-creature ring buffer (24 × 3 floats = 288 bytes/creature) gives users a window into "what is this brain actually doing."

**Effort:** S. Add `recent_actions: [u8; 200]` ring buffer to `CreatureSoA` (or just to the inspector — pull last-action via wasm at 10 Hz and store TS-side). Energy sparkline trivially aggregates on the TS side over the last 60 frames.

**Impact:** M. Hugely improves observability. The "watch a creature live" experience becomes meaningful.

**Sketch:** TS-only path first: poll `creature_inspect_json` at 10 Hz while inspector open; append `current_action` to a TS-side ring; render with Canvas2D. No Rust changes needed for v1.

**Risks/uncertainties:** TS-side polling sees only the action that happens to be current at sample time — biased by the 10 Hz vs sim-100x speed. Better path adds a Rust-side ring buffer per inspected creature. Defer to "if v1 isn't enough."

### U2. Population + grass-density combined chart

**What:** Add a second line (grass total density) to the existing population chart. Optionally a third for "live grass cells" (already in perf panel).

**Why now:** Pop and grass are tightly coupled (creatures graze grass; grass grows back). Seeing them on the same axis makes the predator–prey dynamic obvious.

**Effort:** XS. `total_grass_density()` already exists on `WorldHandle`. Just sample alongside `stats_sample()` and add a line to the chart.

**Impact:** M. Reveals the core ecological coupling at a glance.

**Sketch:** In `web/src/rail/stats.ts`, extend the sample tuple to `[tick, pop, grass_total]`. Draw two lines (or a separate panel). Auto-scale.

**Risks/uncertainties:** none.

### U3. Brain visualizer ("the NN as a glyph")

**What:** Inspector adds a small (96 × 96 px) canvas that draws the 24 hidden units × 5 outputs as a heatmap (24 rows, 5 cols, each cell colored by the second-layer weight value). The current activation pattern can be overlaid via a glow.

**Why now:** 24 × 5 = 120 cells is tiny and easy to render. Combined with color hash (24-bit), gives the user two complementary fingerprints of "who is this creature." Brain shape is now fixed, so the visual is stable across creatures.

**Effort:** M. Need to expose the hidden→output weights as a `Float32Array`; render with Canvas2D `putImageData`.

**Impact:** L. This is the single most "demo-able" feature — opens the door to "see the brain change as the lineage evolves." Pairs with E1 + E2 (compare founder lineages by brain glyph).

**Sketch:** New wasm getter `creature_hidden_output_weights(idx: u32) -> Float32Array` (120 floats). TS: render 24 × 5 image, each pixel = weight value mapped through a diverging colormap (e.g. blue–white–red). Repaint on inspector refresh.

**Risks/uncertainties:** weights are unstable per-tick (don't change); cache on inspector open. Hidden-layer activations DO change per tick — separate overlay if desired.

### U4. Hotkey overlay + dev-UX polish

**What:** Press `?` to toggle a small overlay showing all hotkeys: `~` for dev panel, `space` for pause (if added), `1/2/3` for speed, `?` for help. Add visible "currently paused" indicator.

**Why now:** Dev panel hotkey is `~` (discoverable only by reading source). Speed control lives in the top bar but isn't keyboarded.

**Effort:** XS. ~30 LOC of HTML/CSS.

**Impact:** S. Quality of life for the user and anyone they show this to.

**Sketch:** Bottom-right overlay-widget; toggled by `?`. Hardcoded list.

**Risks/uncertainties:** none.

### U5. WebGL2 renderer (revisit)

**What:** Replace `drawCreatures` + `drawGrassMap` Canvas2D path with a WebGL2 instanced-quad renderer.

**Why now:** Original `webgl2-renderer-design.md` was deleted by docs-cleanup. With v1.3's uniform creatures (no per-creature size variation, no rings) and grass as a simple uniform-color grid, an instanced renderer is straightforward. At pop=2000 + grass-cell heatmap, Canvas2D is the bottleneck.

**Effort:** L. Shader setup, instanced vertex buffer for creatures, texture upload for grass density. Need to keep the highlight ring working.

**Impact:** L. 5–10× draw-time improvement at heavy load; unlocks "what if N = 10000?" experiments. Pairs with P1 + P2 (backend gains) to enable larger sims.

**Sketch:** Single `gl.drawArraysInstanced` for circles (geometry shader expands a point to a 4-vert quad with discard-outside-radius in frag). Grass = a `R8` texture, uploaded once per frame from `grass_buffer()`. Highlight ring: separate small instanced call.

**Risks/uncertainties:** WebGL2 + COOP/COEP + Vite worker integration. Bundle size growth. Maintainability of a shader layer (Canvas2D is simpler for casual contributors). Big commitment.

## 4. Other big ideas

### O1. Headless training / batch mode

**What:** A `cargo run --bin headless --release -- --seed X --ticks 50000 --out csv` binary that runs the sim with no wasm, dumps `(tick, pop, mean_age, mean_energy, grass_total, dominant_lineage)` to CSV.

**Why now:** TPS at 8000+ means a 50k-tick run takes 6 seconds native. Batch sweeps over (seed × mutation_rate × grass_growth) are entirely tractable. Currently impossible without a browser.

**Effort:** S. Add `src/bin/headless.rs`; reuse `World` directly (no `WorldHandle` wrapper). Use `clap` (already a transitive dep?) or roll-your-own arg parsing.

**Impact:** L. Enables real experimental work on the sim. Pairs with E4 (mutation sweeps) and E5 (selection temperature sweeps).

**Sketch:** Loop `world.tick_once()`; every K ticks emit a row. Optional `--quiet` flag for raw speed.

**Risks/uncertainties:** native-target builds may need feature-gating around `wasm-bindgen`-only code in `wasm_api.rs`. Already works for `cargo test --lib` so should be fine.

### O2. Save/replay (minimal — seed + tick + sliders)

**What:** Replace the deleted F.26 IDB persistence with a one-shot serialize/deserialize: `(seed, tick, dev_sliders) → fresh world fast-forwarded to that tick`. Reproducible because the sim is deterministic from seed.

**Why now:** Q4 deleted save entirely. Users now have no way to capture/share an interesting state. A "share this seed + tick + slider config" URL would re-create the run.

**Effort:** S. URL params: `?seed=X&tick=Y&sliders=base64(json)`. On boot, run `world.step_n(Y)` to fast-forward (15s wall time at 8000 TPS for 100k ticks is acceptable).

**Impact:** M. Solves the "I saw something cool, how do I show you" problem. Doesn't reintroduce IDB, schema versioning, autosave, or any of the complexity D1 deleted.

**Sketch:** Strip-down `web/src/main.ts` URL-param read; build a copy-link button next to the seed pill. Fast-forward with a "loading…" overlay.

**Risks/uncertainties:** fast-forward at speed=N takes wall-clock time; cap at a max-fast-forward (e.g. 200k ticks).

### O3. Multi-world / side-by-side parallel populations

**What:** Render 2 (or 4) independent `WorldHandle`s in a tiled view; users see "same sliders, different seeds" or "different sliders, same seed" evolve in parallel.

**Why now:** With threads on and a fast sim, running 4 worlds is feasible. Comparison is the natural way to study evolutionary dynamics — currently impossible.

**Effort:** L. Significant UI work (canvas tiling, per-world inspector dispatch, per-world dev panel). Sim layer needs minor work (multiple `WorldHandle` instances should already work — `World::new` is stateless).

**Impact:** L. Unique feature that demos extremely well.

**Sketch:** Add a "split" toggle in top bar; 4 canvases in a 2×2 grid; shared dev panel applies to all OR per-world panel.

**Risks/uncertainties:** Canvas2D rendering 4 worlds at 60 FPS may need O.5 (render-skip on inactive worlds) or P5 (WebGL2).

### O4. WebGPU compute for NN forward (way out of scope flag)

**What:** Move the `Brain::forward` pass into a WGSL compute shader; one workgroup per creature.

**Why now:** Marking as a "horizon" item — not for v1.4 — but worth flagging now that we have a fixed-shape NN.

**Effort:** XL. WebGPU is still browser-limited (Firefox, Safari iffy). Requires new build pipeline.

**Impact:** XL **if** the project ever wants pop = 10000+. Otherwise YAGNI.

**Sketch:** Don't.

**Risks/uncertainties:** browser support, build complexity, complete divergence from current Rust/wasm path. Flag-only; do not start.

### O5. Touch / mobile support

**What:** Make the canvas pan/zoom work with touch gestures; resize the rail to a bottom drawer on narrow viewports.

**Why now:** Sim already runs fine in mobile browsers (modulo SAB); UI doesn't.

**Effort:** M. Touch event mapping; responsive CSS pass; verify wasm-bindgen-rayon falls through cleanly to single-threaded on mobile (it does).

**Impact:** M. Wider audience. Useful for "show this to a friend" demos.

**Sketch:** `pointerdown/move/up` already work for mouse via Pointer Events; add pinch-zoom; reorganize rail to bottom drawer with `@media (max-width: 800px)`.

**Risks/uncertainties:** performance on low-end phones may force a sliders-only "headless render" mode.

## Recommended v1.4 shape

Pick a small, coherent set that targets the actual user-visible weaknesses of v1.3:

1. **E1 — Multi-founder seeding** (XS effort, L impact). Fixes the "80 % of tabs are dead in 60 s" problem. Required to make any of the rest of v1.4 feel good.
2. **U2 — Population + grass combined chart** (XS effort, M impact). Makes the founder/grass dynamic legible.
3. **P1 — Brain weight transpose** (M effort, L impact). Single biggest perf win; lets us push pop higher without sliders.
4. **P3 — Render spans + creature batch fills** (S effort, M impact). Trims render to make the visible aquarium feel smooth at heavy pop.
5. **U3 — Brain visualizer in inspector** (M effort, L impact). The "demo-able" feature; gives the inspector a real reason to exist.

**Why this set:** It addresses each of the four categories with a balanced load (1 evolution, 1 UI viz, 1 backend perf, 1 frontend perf, 1 deep UI feature). Total effort sums to ~S + M + S + M + XS ≈ 1.5–2 weeks. No proposal here contradicts a locked Q-amendment. None reintroduce deleted systems (no genome, no species, no save). Together they cover the three loudest weaknesses of post-v1.3 main: opacity, fragility, and "render feels slow even though sim is fast."

**Stretch (if v1.4 has room):** add **O1 (headless training mode)** — XS-to-S effort, unlocks an entire research workflow at almost zero cost. **E3 (sexual reproduction)** is the most evolution-interesting deferred idea but has design surface; better as v1.5.

**Deferred to v1.5+:** WebGL2 renderer (U5), multi-world (O3), WebGPU compute (O4), mobile (O5), crossover (E3). All require committed time and a stable v1.4 baseline first.
